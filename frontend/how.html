How it works

The why, not the what.

A voice agent that logs your engineering day to Proof — designed around one rule and one insight.

The one rule

The model never touches your transcript. Speech-to-text produces your raw words; they're stored unchanged and are exactly what gets posted. The language model only ever reads them — to pick the next question and to label the verb. That boundary is enforced in code: the conversation engine owns the transcripts and is the only thing that can write them.

The interview

One fixed opener — "what did you work on today?" Everything after is generated. After each answer the planner reads the whole conversation and asks the single best next question, quoting your own words, and decides on its own when it has enough.

It's steered toward what a strong log needs, from Proof's public corpus: a reason, the alternative you chose it over, and one honest thing — what you skipped or got wrong. 19% of picked logs name an alternative, 34% fill that honest line, 53% name a concrete artefact.

Turn-taking

It waits while you think — a pause doesn't cut you off — reads its question aloud, and won't talk over you. Tap I'm done to end a turn, pause or stop any time, or type instead.

Ten languages

English, Tamil, Hindi, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi. The question, the transcription, and the voice all follow the language you pick.

Your memory, your identity

Recall is scoped to you. By default an anonymous per-browser key; pair an authenticator app (TOTP) once and your history follows you to any device — no password, no email. The 6-digit code is only ever verified, never stored.

The stack

FastAPI · Groq Whisper large-v3 (speech) · gpt-oss-120b (adaptive planner) · ElevenLabs (voice) · Postgres (recall) · posts to Proof via its MCP connector. Runs at zero key-cost in a browser-only fallback; upgrades with keys.
